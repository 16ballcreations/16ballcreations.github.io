Modularity, an idea that came back stronger

A system for business owners who aren't technical: orders, bookings, stock and invoices in one place, speaking each business's language.

Where it started

Where it came from

The idea was born in 2015: a system that would put a business's whole day in one place. The first version was built with Angular and a PHP backend, and it was lost. All that remained was a document with its structure.

The idea, though, wasn't lost. Many businesses still run their day on notebooks, spreadsheets and chats, and existing systems speak the software's language, not the business's. That's why today we're building it again, with a clearer vision.

The decisions

Why we did it this way

  1. Starting over without forgetting

    From the first Modularity, the document of its structure remained. We use it as memory, not as a mold: the new version starts from what we've learned over these years about how a business really works.

  2. One core for many businesses

    A coffee shop, a repair shop, a salon and a clinic share more than it seems. Modularity has a single core that adapts to each one, turning on only what that business needs.

  3. Each business's own words

    A coffee shop talks about tables; a repair shop, about vehicles; a clinic, about patients. The system changes its words so every owner feels at home.

  4. Made for people who aren't technical

    It's built for the owner who serves, charges and decides. If you need to read a manual to use it, something went wrong.

  5. The books aren't erased

    Billing keeps the full trail of every movement. What happened with the money stays written and isn't rewritten.

  6. Tested before promised

    Every part of the system has automated tests, over 400 in the core, so what works today keeps working tomorrow.
